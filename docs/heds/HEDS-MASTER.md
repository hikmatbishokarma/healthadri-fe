## 1. HEDS — HealthAdri Emotional Design System

### Mission
HealthAdri quietly walks beside people navigating cancer. Not in front. Not behind. Beside.

### Product Promise
We do not promise outcomes. We promise companionship.

### Emotional North Star
REASSURANCE.

### 3-Second Rule
Every HealthAdri illustration and important emotional UI moment must communicate emotional safety within three seconds.

The patient should not need to analyze the interface. They should simply feel:
"Someone is with me." / "I'm okay."

Review:
- What did I notice first?
- What did I feel?
- What story did I understand?
- Did I need to interpret anything?
- Did illness become the first association?

The emotional response must happen before the cognitive response.

### Quiet Companion Principle
Nothing should make the patient feel lonely: illustrations, empty states, copy, animations, onboarding, errors, waiting states.

### Core Principles
1. Reassurance before visual excitement.
2. Nothing should feel lonely.
3. The relationship is the emotional hero.
4. The person comes before the environment.
5. Emotion comes from posture, not facial expression.
6. Support is shown, never announced.
7. Illness is never the hero.
8. One illustration = one micro-story.
9. Environment supports the story and never competes.
10. Every object must earn its place.
11. Hope is implied, never promised.
12. The patient should feel accompanied, not reminded of illness.
13. Emotion comes from composition, not rendering.
14. Illustrations communicate presence, not perfection.
15. Preserve user familiarity; improve emotional quality.

### Other Rules
- Recognition before creativity.
- Calm before beautiful.
- Write like a trusted companion, never like a system.
- Motion should breathe: no fast, jumping, shouting animation.
- Never reference another brand as the target visual style; describe qualities and behavior instead.

### One-sentence design principle
"We design experiences that quietly reassure people during one of the most difficult journeys of their lives."

## 2. HealthAdri Illustration DNA v1.0

### Emotional Foundation
Core emotion: Reassurance
Primary feeling: "Someone is with me."
Secondary feeling: Calm confidence.

Never make the primary emotional goal excitement, celebration, urgency, fear, or pity.

### Storytelling
Every illustration tells ONE micro-story, expressible in one sentence.

### Hero Hierarchy
Relationship -> Person -> Action -> Environment

The relationship is always the emotional hero.

### Characters
Universal, warm, approachable, quiet, dignified, simplified, timeless.

Avoid cartoon exaggeration, fashion styling, perfect beauty, hyper-realistic anatomy, childishness, heroic poses.

### Emotional Expression
Use posture, gesture, distance, body orientation, shared movement and rhythm.

Avoid huge smiles, crying, thumbs up, cheerleading and exaggerated happiness.

### Companion Principle
Support is shown, never announced.

Good: walking together, sitting together, listening, shared silence, gentle hand placement, shared direction.

Avoid explanatory labels such as Hope, Strength, Wellbeing.

### Environment
Only support the story. Possible elements: soft path, gentle horizon, abstract hills, organic botanical forms, open sky.

Avoid hospitals as focal points, medical equipment, clinical rooms, needles, busy cities, crowds, large buildings, decorative flowers, decorative birds and detailed landscapes.

### Path
A path represents journey, not destination.

### Colour
Muted, warm, soft, low-saturation, low-contrast. Directions may include sage green, dusty lavender, warm peach, soft blue, warm ivory.

### Rendering
Minimal Editorial Vector:
- flat colour blocks
- soft organic geometry
- minimal depth
- SVG-friendly
- animation-ready

Avoid clay, glossy 3D, painterly rendering, realistic textures and heavy gradients.

### Composition
Approx. 70% emotional weight on characters/relationship; approx. 30% environment. Target 40–50% breathing room.

### Camera
Preferred: three-quarter side, three-quarter rear, eye level.
Avoid: top-down, heroic low angle, dramatic perspective, full back view, extreme close-up.

### Motion readiness
Gentle breathing, subtle sway, soft floating, slow fade, tiny hand/posture movement.

### Accessibility
Story should work without text, at small mobile size, in grayscale and for first-time users.

## 4. HealthAdri Master Style Specification v1.0 — LOCKED

### Purpose
Illustrations are emotional companions, not decorative assets. They reduce emotional burden and quietly communicate that no one has to face their journey alone.

### Emotional Contract
"The patient should never feel reminded of their illness more than they feel supported."

### Emotional Objective
One outcome only: reassurance, presence, calm, guidance, connection, confidence, relief, or quiet encouragement.

### Storytelling
One micro-story. If explanation is required, simplify.

Relationship -> Person -> Action -> Environment.

Warm, quiet, respectful, human, simplified, timeless. Never heroic, childish, fashion-focused, hyper-realistic or overly expressive.

Posture, gesture, distance, body orientation, rhythm. Not exaggerated facial expression.

Supports the emotional story and never becomes the hero.

### Path
Journey, not destination.

### Colour
Muted, warm, soft, low saturation, low contrast.

Minimal editorial vector, SVG-friendly, rounded organic geometry, minimal depth. No clay, painterly rendering, heavy gradients, gloss or texture-heavy rendering.

### Composition
Relationship ≈70% emotional weight; environment ≈30%; whitespace ≈40–50%.

### Camera
Preferred: three-quarter side, three-quarter rear, eye level. Avoid dramatic/full-back/top-down views.

### Object Audit
If removing an object does not change the story, remove it.

### Accessibility
Understandable without text, on small mobile, in grayscale and by first-time users.

### Motion
Calm, slow, subtle.

### Review Gate
Must pass:
1. Three-second understanding.
2. Emotional safety.
3. Relationship-first hierarchy.
4. One-sentence story.
5. Environment does not compete.
6. Nothing feels lonely.
7. Illness is not the hero.

### Design Oath
"Every HealthAdri illustration should make the patient feel accompanied, understood, and a little calmer—without ever needing to say those words."

### Mandatory question
"If this were the only thing a patient saw today, would they feel less alone?"

## 5. HealthAdri Master Style Prompt v1.0

Use this as the parent prompt for every illustration, together with that illustration's Blueprint.

You are the lead editorial illustrator for HealthAdri.

HealthAdri is a quiet companion for people navigating cancer. It is not a hospital, treatment, or clinical application in its emotional identity.

Your job is not to make decorative artwork. Your job is to reduce emotional burden through visual storytelling.

Default emotional target: quiet reassurance. The patient should quietly feel: "Someone is with me."

Do not promise healing, recovery, success or any specific outcome.

Within three seconds:
- the emotional story must be understandable
- the relationship must be noticed before the environment
- no symbol should require decoding
- the patient should feel calmer or accompanied
- illness should not become the first association

Create exactly one micro-story.

Visual hierarchy: Relationship -> Person -> Action -> Environment.

Use universal, warm, dignified, simplified, rounded editorial characters. Communicate emotion through posture, gesture, body orientation, distance, shared movement and rhythm.

Use only the minimum environment required to support the story.

Target rendering: Minimal Editorial Vector, flat colour blocks, rounded organic geometry, restrained depth, clean silhouettes, SVG-friendly and animation-ready.

Use muted, warm, soft, low-saturation, low-contrast colours.

Prefer three-quarter side, three-quarter rear or eye-level camera.

Every object must earn its place. If removing it does not change the story, remove it.

Output: high resolution, clean silhouettes, SVG-friendly, animation-ready, no text, no logos, no UI elements.

Never create hospitals as focal points, cancer as visual hero, medical equipment as decoration, fear, pity, dramatic illness symbolism, confetti, trophies, busy scenery, decorative clutter, clay aesthetics, painterly aesthetics, glossy 3D, stock-illustration feel, exaggerated cartooning, environment dominating people, destination dominating relationship, or dramatic sunrise as a promise of hope.

Final requirement: the illustration must make the patient feel accompanied, understood and a little calmer without explicitly saying those words.

## 8. Safe Beginning — Illustration #001 Benchmark Candidate

Micro-story:
"Someone is beside you as you take the first step."

### What the iterations taught us
Version 1: Hospital became the hero. Lesson: illness/healthcare infrastructure must never be the visual hero.

Version 2: Hospital disappeared, but sunrise/destination became the hero. Lesson: replacing a medical destination with a hopeful destination does not solve the underlying issue.

Version 3: Clay/3D rendering became the focus. Lesson: emotion should come from composition and relationship, not rendering.

Final benchmark direction:
- two people
- quiet companionship
- three-quarter perspective
- relationship first
- minimal environment
- subtle path
- muted palette
- flat editorial vector language
- emotion primarily through posture
- no visible medical reminder
- no dramatic promise of hope

Core lesson:
"The relationship is the hero."

## 9. Applying HEDS to the Existing Application

DO NOT rewrite the application.

DO NOT redesign every screen from scratch.

DO NOT change working architecture or user mental models without evidence.

Use:
Existing screen -> HEDS audit -> emotional debt -> smallest meaningful improvement -> review -> ship.

### Emotional debt
A screen can technically work while creating unnecessary emotional friction:
- cold system language
- alarming empty state
- abrupt animation
- generic illustration
- unclear next step
- excessive visual noise
- unnecessary medical emphasis

### Severity
RED = anxiety, loneliness, confusion or significant emotional overload.
YELLOW = generic/cold communication or inconsistency.
GREEN = cosmetic polish.

Fix RED first.

# 10. Claude Code Operating Procedure

## Phase A — Understand (NO CODE CHANGES)
1. Inspect the existing repository.
2. Identify the patient-facing application.
3. Map routes/screens/components.
4. Understand existing design tokens.
5. Understand navigation and current user flows.
6. Identify reusable UI primitives.
7. Do not modify code yet.

## Phase B — Audit
Create a screen-by-screen audit containing:
- screen purpose
- patient moment
- likely emotional state
- current strengths
- HEDS issues
- emotional debt
- severity
- minimal recommended change

## Phase C — Prioritize
Do not fix everything. Prioritize high-impact emotional debt.

## Phase D — Implement
Implement one small batch at a time.
Preserve existing architecture, working functionality, user mental models and navigation unless there is evidence of a problem.

## Phase E — Review
Review changed screens against:
- HEDS
- three-second rule where applicable
- emotional safety
- cognitive load
- relationship-first illustration language

## Phase F — Learn
Record repeated observations. Do not modify HEDS from a single subjective preference.
